Gin Rummy Rules

Get your cards into melds and your deadwood count down before your opponent does.

  • Two players, one standard 52-card deck
  • Cards rank King (high) down to Ace (low)
  • Card values: Aces are 1, face cards are 10, everything else is its number
  • Pick the first dealer any way you like; the deal then alternates every hand
  • The dealer gives each player 10 cards, one at a time, starting with the opponent
  • The next card goes face up to start the discard pile; the rest are the face-down stock
  • First turn: the non-dealer may take the face-up card or pass. If they pass, the dealer may take it or pass. If both pass, the non-dealer draws from the stock
  • Set: 3 or 4 cards of the same rank, like 7-7-7
  • Run: 3 or more cards in a row in the same suit, like 4-5-6 of hearts
  • Aces are low only: A-2-3 is a run, Q-K-A is not
  • A card can only count in one meld at a time
  • Deadwood is every card not in a meld; its count is the total of those cards’ values
  • Draw the top card of the stock or the top card of the discard pile
  • Then discard one card face up
  • If you took the top discard, you can’t throw that same card back this turn
  • Once your deadwood count is 10 or less, you may knock to end the hand: discard face down (or just say so)
  • Both players lay out their melds and deadwood
  • The other player may then lay off deadwood by adding it to the knocker’s melds; the knocker can’t lay off onto theirs
  • If the stock gets down to its last two cards with no knock, the hand is a draw and nobody scores
  • Going gin: knocking with no deadwood at all. The other player can’t lay off
  • Big gin: instead of going gin, keep playing until the card you pick up melds too, so all 11 cards are in melds. Again, no laying off
  • Knock: if the knocker’s deadwood is lower, the knocker scores the difference
  • Undercut: if the other player’s deadwood is equal or lower (after laying off), they score the difference plus a 25-point bonus
  • Gin: 25 points plus the other player’s full deadwood count
  • Big gin: 31 points plus the other player’s full deadwood count
  • Keep a running total; the first player to reach 100 (or whatever you agreed) ends the game
  • Game bonus: the winner gets 100 extra points
  • Box bonus (some variations): each player adds 25 points for every hand they won
  • Shutout: if the winner won every hand, their hand points are doubled before the box bonus is added

Summarized from the Wikipedia article Gin rummy (as edited September 14, 2026).

Cancellation Rules

A variant for bigger tables. Here's exactly what it changes from standard Hearts.

  • One 52-card deck, best with exactly 4 players
  • Each Heart is worth 1 point, the Queen of Spades is worth 13 — 26 points are in play each hand
  • Players must follow the suit led if they can
  • No point cards may be played on the first trick, and Hearts can't be led until they've been "broken" (played off-suit earlier in the hand)
  • Lowest total score wins once someone reaches 100
  • Two full 52-card decks are shuffled together (104 cards), so 5–7+ players can each still get a full, even hand
  • Since every card now has a duplicate, if the exact same card is played twice in one trick (e.g. both Jacks of Clubs), that pair cancels out — neither one counts toward winning the trick
  • If every card played to a trick cancels out, the whole trick carries over and gets added to the next trick instead of being collected
  • Scoring is otherwise unchanged: Hearts = 1 point, Queen of Spades = 13, lowest total still wins

Every player needs the same number of cards, and one unique card has to lead the first trick. So depending on the table size, a joker is added and/or some 2s are removed.
